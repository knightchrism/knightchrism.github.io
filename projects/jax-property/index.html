Case study · PostGIS · dbt · Leaflet

Jacksonville Property & Flood Risk

Duval County parcels, assessor records, FEMA flood zones and Florida sales data joined in one tested PostGIS database, explorable on an interactive map.

Live demo Repository

Problem

Property questions (what is this parcel worth, is it in a flood zone, what sold nearby) need county parcels, the assessor tax roll, FEMA flood zones and state sales records joined together. Those sources arrive as shapefiles, pipe-delimited text and REST services, with invalid geometries, duplicate addresses and inconsistent ZIPs. The goal: one trustworthy PostGIS database, without silently losing a row.

Data sources

  • Duval County parcel GIS (Parcels.shp), City of Jacksonville / Duval County public records
  • Duval County Property Appraiser real estate tax roll
  • FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer (public domain), from FEMA's ArcGIS REST service
  • Florida Department of Revenue sales data file and land-use codes

Key decisions

  • Repair geometry with make_valid, never edit the source. Invalid/missing geometry counts are printed before load.
  • One projected CRS: EPSG:2236 (Florida East, US feet), so parcel and flood-zone areas and overlay percentages are meaningful.
  • Raw → clean → QC tables for flood zones. Every repaired shape is compared to its raw original by area.
  • Flood overlay per parcel. Each parcel gets the FEMA zones it touches, whether each is a Special Flood Hazard Area, and the percent of the parcel covered.
  • dbt-tested models with 13 data tests on every build.
  • No owner names published. The public explorer shows address, market and assessed value only.
